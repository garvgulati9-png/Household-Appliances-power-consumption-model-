1. Executive Summary

This investigation delivers a rigorous comparative benchmark between the machine learning algorithms proposed in the G1 Research Paper (Machaca-Casani et al., Electric Power Systems Research, Vol. 252, 2026) and our team's Stacked Hybrid Super-Learner Architecture across two vastly different data-scale regimes. We resolve four fundamental engineering challenges:

  1. Evaluating G1 Methods on High-Frequency Telemetry: Training G1's models (Linear Regression, Support Vector Regression with RBF kernel, Random Forest, and Deep Neural Network MLP 50x50) on our high-volume smart home telemetry dataset (19,735 observations, 50 engineered features).
  2. Evaluating Our Model on Small-Sample Urban Data: Training our Stacked Hybrid Ensemble (ExtraTrees + XGBoost + HistGradientBoosting with non-negative Ridge meta-weights) on G1's 200-sample urban survey cohort from Piedra Santa (Arequipa, Peru).
  3. Explaining Dataset Incompatibility & Why Models Cannot Directly Cross-Predict: Detailing the physical unit disparity (Watt-hours per 10 minutes vs. active Kilowatts), orthogonal feature matrices (50 room sensors vs. 9 demographic indicators), and structural differences (single-dwelling longitudinal time-series vs. multi-dwelling cross-sectional survey).
  4. Algorithmic Degradation Flagging & Automated Fallback: Implementing an automated safety guardrail that detects when any model produces a negative coefficient of determination (R² < 0), diagnoses the underlying over-parameterization, flags the model, and automatically falls back to our robust baseline method.

2. Scientific Rationale: Why Did We Apply Our Stacked Model on the 200-Data Dataset?

Our Stacked Hybrid Super-Learner was initially engineered and optimized for large-scale, high-frequency telemetry (19,735 records), where it successfully achieved state-of-the-art performance (R² = 75.04%). Testing our stacked ensemble on the micro-scale 200-sample dataset was conducted for three explicit scientific reasons:

  • 1. Stress-Testing Cross-Scale Generalization: A critical open question in machine learning is whether an advanced ensemble architecture that dominates large-volume data retains its predictive capability when transferred without modification to a data-constrained urban environment (sample size N = 200).
  • 2. Empirical Validation of the Complexity-Volume Dilemma: Statistical learning theory posits that model capacity must remain proportional to sample degrees of freedom. By running our stacked ensemble (comprising hundreds of decision trees across three distinct algorithms: ExtraTrees, XGBoost, and HistGradientBoosting) on only 140 training observations, we empirically tested the exact point at which structural complexity triggers variance explosion and negative predictive performance.
  • 3. Empirical Evidence Justifying the Automated Fallback Guardrail: The experiment produced an empirical result of R² = -0.1205 for our complex tree ensemble on the 200 dataset. This negative score provided the quantitative justification needed to design and enforce our automated guardrail protocol: when sample sizes drop below 1,000 observations or when R² falls below zero, the system must disengage complex multi-model stacking and fall back to regularized linear or kernel baselines (such as SVR or Linear Regression).

3. Detailed Comparison: Our Method vs. G1 PDF Methods

3.1 Our Method: Stacked Hybrid Super-Learner & Multi-Tier Anomaly Engine

Our architecture formulates residential energy forecasting as a multi-tier learning hierarchy:

  • Base Learners (Level 0): An ensemble of three complementary non-linear tree algorithms:
    • ExtraTrees Regressor: 400 extremely randomized trees utilizing random cut-point thresholds to maximize variance reduction.
    • XGBoost Regressor: 700 gradient-boosted decision trees (maximum depth = 8, learning rate = 0.03) incorporating L1 and L2 regularization to penalize leaf weight magnitude.
    • HistGradientBoosting Regressor: 500 iterations of histogram-binned gradient boosting for rapid convergence on continuous feature distributions.
  • Meta-Learner (Level 1): Out-of-fold predictions from the base learners are blended through a non-negative Ridge Regression meta-model (alpha = 1.0, weights constrained to be greater than or equal to 0). On the 19,735 dataset, the learned optimal blending weights are 48.1% ExtraTrees, 34.3% XGBoost, and 17.6% HistGradientBoosting.
  • Multi-Tier Anomaly Detection Engine: A two-stage operational diagnostic screening module:
    • Tier 1: Unsupervised Isolation Forest (250 isolation trees, contamination rate = 2.5%) measuring path-length isolation in multivariate sensor space.
    • Tier 2: Supervised dynamic residual thresholding based on a 3-IQR (interquartile range) and 2-sigma deviation envelope.
    • Domain Alert Categorization: Automated tagging into actionable categories: Vampire Night Load, Critical Spike Runaway, and Sudden Drop Malfunction.

3.2 G1 PDF Methods (Machaca-Casani et al., 2026)

The G1 methodology benchmarked four standalone traditional and machine learning algorithms on a 200-sample urban cohort:

  • Linear Regression (Ordinary Least Squares): Classical parametric baseline optimizing the residual sum of squares across 9 demographic predictors.
  • Support Vector Regression (SVR with RBF Kernel): Non-linear kernel regression solving the dual quadratic optimization problem with penalty parameter C = 1.0 and insensitivity tube width ε = 0.1.
  • Random Forest Regressor: 100 decorrelated decision trees using bootstrap aggregation (bagging) with random feature subspace selection.
  • Deep Neural Network (MLP 50x50): Feedforward multilayer perceptron with an input layer, two fully connected hidden layers containing 50 ReLU neurons each, and a linear output unit (optimized via Adam with squared error loss).
  • Validation Framework: 70/30 train/test partition (140 train, 60 test) combined with 5-fold cross-validation and a One-Way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) hypothesis test across validation folds.

4. Why We Cannot Predict Those Values & Comprehensive Dataset Differences

A primary technical question is: Why can a model trained on our original dataset not directly predict the values of the G1 dataset (and vice versa)? The explanation lies in three fundamental structural and physical incompatibilities:

Comparison Dimension Dataset 1: Our Original Dataset (Belgium Smart Home) Dataset 2: G1 Dataset (Piedra Santa, Peru) Incompatibility Analysis
Physical Target & Units Appliances measured in Watt-hours (Wh) per 10-minute interval (Range: 10 to 120 Wh, Mean: 68.2 Wh). Energy Consumption (kW) measured in instantaneous Kilowatts (kW) active power (Range: 0.61 to 4.31 kW, Mean: 2.492 kW). Physical & Numerical Incompatibility: One Watt-hour is a cumulative measure of energy over time (1 Wh = 3,600 Joules), whereas one Kilowatt is instantaneous power draw (1 kW = 1,000 Joules/sec). A model calibrated to predict 68 Wh cannot output a 2.5 kW demand figure.
Input Feature Space 50 continuous telemetry features: 9 room temperatures (T1–T9), 9 room humidities (RH_1–RH_9), atmospheric pressure (mm Hg), outdoor wind speed, visibility, and dew point. 9 socio-demographic survey features: Diurnal hour, outdoor temperature (°C), family size (1–6 people), dwelling type (Apartment/Duplex/House), AC presence, and appliance usage intensity (Low/Moderate). Disjoint Vector Spaces: The mathematical input matrices are completely orthogonal. Dataset 1 contains zero information on family size or housing type; Dataset 2 contains zero sensor readings for indoor rooms, relative humidity, or barometric pressure.
Observational Sampling Regime High-frequency longitudinal time-series: 19,735 readings collected continuously at 10-minute intervals over 4.5 months for one single residence. Discrete cross-sectional survey: 200 static household records sampled across 200 different families and physical structures at specific times of day. Temporal vs. Spatial Variation: Dataset 1 models internal thermal inertia, weather hysteresis, and routine daily habits within one physical structure. Dataset 2 models architectural diversity, family demographic differences, and socio-economic habits across an entire neighborhood.

7. Root-Cause Analysis: Why Did Models Yield R² < 0 on the Small Dataset?

The coefficient of determination is mathematically defined as:

R² = 1 - [ Mean Squared Error of Model / Variance of Actual Target ]
When R² < 0: MSE(Model) > Var(Actual Target)

A negative R² indicates that the model's predictions produce larger squared error than a simple flat reference line predicting the historical sample mean (ȳ = 2.492 kW). Our diagnosis isolates two distinct degradation catalysts:

  • Deep MLP Collapse (R² = -0.7941): The dual 50-neuron MLP contains over 3,100 free synaptic parameters (10 x 50 + 50 x 50 + 50 x 1). Optimizing 3,100 weights on only 140 training observations produces an extreme degree-of-freedom deficit (0.045 observations per parameter). The network memorizes training sample noise rather than generalizable relationships, causing large residual spikes on unseen test points.
  • Tree Ensemble Degradation (R² = -0.0532 to -0.1205): Decision trees recursively partition feature space into fine-grained leaves. In the presence of 10.5% behavioral outliers (such as midnight air conditioning surges), unconstrained trees carve out high-variance decision regions that over-predict normal households during testing.
  • Why SVR and Linear Regression Resist Degradation: SVR utilizes Structural Risk Minimization bounded by an ε-insensitive tube (penalty parameter C = 1.0, tube width ε = 0.1). Residuals smaller than ε receive zero penalty, and the RBF kernel enforces global hyperplane smoothness, preventing the boundary from distorting around local outliers. Linear Regression is structurally constrained to only 10 parameters (p + 1 = 10), preventing variance runaway.

8. Automated Guardrail Protocol: Flag and Fallback to Robust Method

To guarantee production reliability, our platform implements an automated architectural safety switchboard:

OPERATIONAL GUARDRAIL SWITCHBOARD LOGIC:

RULE 1 (Micro-Scale Trigger):
IF (Sample Count N < 1,000) OR (Hold-out Validation R² < 0):
  • Issue Alert: [FLAG: MODEL_DEGRADATION_DETECTED]
  • Immediately disengage Deep Neural Networks and Multi-Tree Stacking.
  • AUTOMATIC FALLBACK: Route all inference requests to Support Vector Regression (SVR with RBF kernel) or Linear Regression (OLS).

RULE 2 (High-Frequency Telemetry Trigger):
ELSE IF (Sample Count N ≥ 10,000):
  • Issue Status: [STATUS: ADVANCED_PIPELINE_ACTIVE]
  • Activate Our Stacked Hybrid Super-Learner (ETR + XGB + HistGBM).
  • Enable Multi-Tier Anomaly Diagnostics (Isolation Forest + Dynamic 3-IQR Residuals).
